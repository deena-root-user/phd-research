🎓 Section 1: Dissertation Defense Strategy & Committee Claims

Reviewer Defense & Dissertation Performance Transition Narrative

A central defense milestone is explaining why Paper 1 & Paper 2 achieved ~98% static accuracy, whereas the flagship ATMIE-Upgrade framework reports 95.34% overall out-of-time accuracy across 7 future eras:

  • The Fallacy of Static 98% Accuracy (In-Time Data Leakage): In Paper 1 (MHSAFS-RAMD) and Paper 2 (IATDL-IAMD), models were evaluated using random 70:30 train/test splits on static datasets (CICMalDroid 2020 & AndroMD). Random splits mix samples from identical malware families across train and test sets, causing artificial high accuracy through signature memorization rather than learning true Android OS evolution.
  • Catastrophic Degradation of Legacy Models Under Out-of-Time Splits: When legacy models (LightGBM, XGBoost, Standard Transformer, DAHT) are subjected to a realistic longitudinal split (train exclusively on 2013–2018 past data, test on 2019–2025 unseen future eras), they collapse from >94% F1 down to <0.50 F1 (0.4985 F1 in 2025)—performing worse than random guessing due to concept drift.
  • ATMIE-Upgrade Zero-Day Resilience Victory: ATMIE-Upgrade is the only framework that survives 12 years of OS evolution, sustaining 95.34% Out-of-Time Accuracy, 0.9639 Macro F1, 0.9489 MCC, and 0.9675 Precision. Under severe micro-malware prevalence in recent eras (2024–2025), ATMIE-Upgrade's Dynamic Threshold Calibration ($\tau^*(t)$) recalibrates decision boundaries, reaching 99.38% accuracy (0.9850 F1) in 2024 and 99.79% accuracy (0.9910 F1) in 2025 where static models completely fail!
📐 Section 2: Mathematical Formulation of ATMIE-Upgrade
MODULE 2.1

Gated Domain Projector (GLU)

For feature subset $g_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ belonging to domain $k$:

$$z_k = \text{LayerNorm}\Big( \big(W_{v,k} g_k + b_{v,k}\big) \odot \sigma(W_{g,k} g_k + b_{g,k}) + 0.1 R_k g_k \Big) \cdot \text{GELU}$$
Projects 4,561 features into 8 domain tokens ($S=128$), reducing memory usage from >30 GB to <500 MB RAM.
MODULE 2.2

Temporal Evolution Encoder (TEE)

For sample year $t$:

$$TE(t) = \text{LayerNorm}\Big(\text{Tanh}\big( W_t [\text{Sinusoidal}(t - t_0) \parallel \text{YearEmb}(t)] + b_t \big)\Big)$$
Injects temporal positional embeddings to track API aging across 12 OS generations ($z_k' = z_k + \text{DomainEmb}(k) + TE(t)$).
MODULE 2.3

Evolution-Aware Attention (EAA)

Multi-Head Self-Attention with LayerScale Modulation:

$$\text{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} \cdot \Big(1 + \alpha \delta(t) + \beta S(f)\Big) \right) V$$
LayerScale parameters $\gamma^{(l)}$ stabilize Transformer gradients under temporal drift scores $\delta(t)$.
MODULE 2.4

Dynamic Threshold Calibration (DTC)

Optimal decision threshold selection per test era $t$:

$$\tau^*(t) = \arg\max_{\tau \in [0.1, 0.9]} \Big(0.7 \cdot \text{Acc}(y_{\text{val}}, \mathbb{I}(p(x) \ge \tau)) + 0.3 \cdot \text{F1}(y_{\text{val}}, \mathbb{I}(p(x) \ge \tau))\Big)$$
Dynamically calibrates decision threshold per test era, elevating overall out-of-time accuracy to 95.34%.

🏛️ Section 8: DAHT Framework (Domain-Aware Hierarchical Transformer)

DAHT Architecture Overview & 12-Year Longitudinal Results

The Domain-Aware Hierarchical Transformer (DAHT) serves as our Stage 3 foundational temporal deep learning architecture. DAHT partitions high-dimensional static feature vectors ($D = 4,561$) into 32 domain tokens of dimension 16, utilizing 4-head multi-layer self-attention to evaluate past-to-future out-of-time concept drift across 12 years of operating system evolution (LAMDA 2013–2025).

  • Hierarchical Token Projection: Converts 4,561 static Android features into 32 structured token sequences ($S=32, D=16$), eliminating linear feature squashing and preserving semantic domain boundaries.
  • Longitudinal Performance: Achieves 90.23% overall out-of-time accuracy, 0.7785 Macro F1, 0.7210 MCC, and 0.8845 Precision across 7 unseen future test eras (2019–2025).
  • Feature Attribution Stability Index (FASI): Paired with SHAP explainability to measure feature importance decay across 12 Android OS generations.
DAHT Mathematical Formulation & Tensor Flow
DAHT MODULE 1

Feature Tokenizer Projection

Maps raw feature vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^{4561}$ into sequence tokens:

$$\text{tokens} = \text{Reshape}\Big(W_{\text{proj}} x + b_{\text{proj}}\Big) \in \mathbb{R}^{\text{batch} \times 32 \times 16}$$
Projects 4,561 raw static features into 32 structured sequence tokens.
DAHT MODULE 2

Token Embeddings & Encoders

Token embedding projection + learnable positional encodings:

$$E(x) = \text{TokenEmbed}(\text{tokens}) + P_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{\text{batch} \times 32 \times 64}$$
Linear token projection ($d_{\text{model}}=64$) with 4-head 2-layer Transformer self-attention.
DAHT MODULE 3

Hierarchical Pooling & Logits

Global average pooling over sequence tokens $\rightarrow$ classification MLP:

$$\text{logits} = W_2 \cdot \text{ReLU}\Big(W_1 \cdot \text{MeanPool}(H) + b_1\Big) + b_2$$
Mean pooling aggregates 32 domain token representations into binary malware classification logits.
